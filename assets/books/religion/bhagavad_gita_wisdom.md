# Bhagavad Gita — Key Teachings

## Chapter 2: The Eternal Self
The soul is never born nor dies. It is eternal, unborn, and imperishable. As a person sheds worn-out garments and puts on new ones, so the soul casts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones.

## Chapter 2: On Action
You have the right to work, but never to the fruit of work. You should never engage in action for the sake of reward, nor should you long for inaction.

## Chapter 2: On Equanimity
Perform work in this world as a man established within himself — without selfish attachments, and alike in success and defeat.

## Chapter 3: Karma Yoga (Action)
Action is the product of the qualities inherent in nature. One who is deluded by ego thinks "I am the doer." But the wise one understands that only the qualities of nature act upon the qualities of nature.

## Chapter 3: On Duty
It is better to perform one's own duty imperfectly than to perform another's duty perfectly.

## Chapter 4: On Knowledge
When reverence, devotion, and service are offered to the enlightened, that knowledge becomes complete.

## Chapter 5: On Renunciation
The person who neither rejoices upon obtaining something pleasant nor grieves upon obtaining something unpleasant — who is self-controlled, has realized the Absolute.

## Chapter 6: On Meditation
For one who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends. For one who has failed to do so, the mind remains the greatest enemy.

## Chapter 6: On Solitude
A person should firmly establish themselves in yoga and live in solitude, with the mind controlled, free from desires and possessions.

## Chapter 7: On Wisdom
Of all forms of knowledge, I am the spiritual wisdom. Among many thousand men, perhaps one strives for perfection; and of those who strive, perhaps one in many thousands knows Me in truth.

## Chapter 8: On Devotion
Whoever remembers Me at the time of death, when departing from the body, attains to My nature. There is no doubt about this.

## Chapter 9: On the Royal Secret
This is the most secret knowledge, the most pure, the highest. Once experienced, it bears the fruit of eternal bliss.

## Chapter 10: On Divine Manifestations
I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds. Everything emanates from Me. The wise who know this worship Me with devotion.

## Chapter 11: The Universal Form
Arjuna sees Krishna's universal form — infinite, all-powerful, containing all beings and all time within a single form.

## Chapter 12: On Devotion
The devotees who worship Me with exclusive devotion — they are most closely united with Me.

## Chapter 13: On Knowledge and Action
The body is the field of action. Knowledge is the knower of the field. The wise know this distinction.

## Chapter 14: On the Three Qualities
All of nature is composed of three qualities: sattva (goodness), rajas (passion), and tamas (ignorance). The wise transcend all three.

## Chapter 15: On the Supreme Person
I am the Supreme Person, the source of everything. All beings come from Me. The wise know this and worship Me.

## Chapter 16: On Divine and Demonic Qualities
Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadfastness — these are divine qualities. Hypocrisy, arrogance, pride — these are demonic.

## Chapter 17: On Faith
Faith is of three kinds: sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic. The quality of faith determines the quality of the person.

## Chapter 18: On Liberation
Abandon all dharmas and take refuge in Me alone. I shall liberate you from all sins; do not grieve.

# Universal Spiritual Principles

## All Paths Lead to the Same Truth
Every religion, every spiritual tradition, every path of wisdom ultimately points to the same divine reality. The destination is the same — only the paths differ.

## The Present Moment
Peace is always available in the present moment. Past and future are mental constructs. Now is all there is.

## Detachment
Hold everything lightly. Possessions, relationships, even your own identity — none of these are permanently "yours." Practice non-attachment while still engaging fully.

## Service
Love expressed through action. Serve others not for reward, but because it is the natural expression of a realized soul.

## Self-Knowledge
The greatest knowledge is knowing yourself. Not your name, not your history, but the consciousness that witnesses all experience.

## Compassion
See yourself in every being. Compassion is not pity — it is the recognition of shared humanity.

## Silence
In silence, the mind settles. In the settled mind, truth reveals itself. Practice silence daily.

## Gratitude
Every breath is a gift. Every moment is grace. Gratitude transforms perception.
