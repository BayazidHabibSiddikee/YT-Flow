# The Psychology of Money — Morgan Housel (Key Lessons)

## 1. No One's Crazy
Everyone makes financial decisions based on their unique experience. What seems crazy to you makes sense to someone else. Don't judge — understand.

## 2. Luck and Risk
Success is never solely due to skill. Luck plays a role. Be humble about your wins and compassionate about others' failures.

## 3. Never Enough
The hardest financial skill is knowing when to stop. There's always someone with more. Don't risk what matters for what doesn't.

## 4. Confounding Compounding
$81.5 billion of Warren Buffett's $84.5 billion came after his 65th birthday. Patience is the ultimate edge.

## 5. Getting Wealthy vs. Staying Wealthy
Getting money requires risk-taking. Keeping money requires humility and fear that what you have can be taken away.

## 6. Tails, You Win
You can be wrong half the time and still get rich. The biggest winners make up for all the losses. Focus on the few big wins.

## 7. Freedom
The highest form of wealth is the ability to control your time. Money is a tool for buying time and freedom.

## 8. Man in the Car Paradox
No one admires the man in the car. They imagine themselves in the car. Status goods don't bring lasting satisfaction.

## 9. Wealth Is What You Don't See
True wealth is the money not spent. The luxury car in the driveway is money gone. The stock portfolio is invisible wealth.

## 10. Save Money
You don't need a specific reason to save. Saving is the gap between your ego and your income. Save for the unknown.

## 11. Reasonable > Rational
Being financially rational is impossible. Be reasonable instead — find a plan that lets you sleep at night.

## 12. Surprise!
History is full of surprises. Plan for the unexpected. What happened before might not happen again.

## 13. Room for Error
Always leave margin for error. The world is unpredictable. A small cushion prevents catastrophe.

## 14. Changes
The biggest risk is always what no one sees coming. Don't rely on past patterns. Adapt constantly.

## 15. Nothing Free
Every investment has a cost. High returns come with high volatility. Know what you're paying.

## 16. You & Me
Different investors have different goals. Don't copy someone else's strategy — they have different time horizons and needs.

## 17. The Seduction of Pessimism
Pessimism sounds smarter than optimism. But history favors the optimists. Don't let fear dominate your thinking.

## 18. When You'll Believe Anything
We all want a story that explains the world. Be careful of neat narratives — reality is messy.
