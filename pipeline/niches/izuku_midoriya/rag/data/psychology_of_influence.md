# Thinking, Fast and Slow — Daniel Kahneman (Key Concepts)

## System 1 vs System 2
- **System 1:** Fast, automatic, intuitive, emotional. Handles 95% of daily decisions.
- **System 2:** Slow, deliberate, logical, effortful. Handles complex calculations.

Most of the time, System 1 runs the show. System 2 is lazy and defaults to System 1's suggestions.

## Cognitive Biases

### Anchoring
The first number you hear influences all subsequent judgments. In negotiations, always make the first offer.

### Loss Aversion
Losses hurt 2x more than equivalent gains feel good. People will take bigger risks to avoid losing than to win.

### Availability Heuristic
We judge probability by how easily examples come to mind. Dramatic events feel more common than they are.

### Confirmation Bias
We seek information that confirms what we already believe. Actively seek disconfirming evidence.

### Dunning-Kruger Effect
Incompetent people overestimate their ability. Competent people underestimate theirs. Humility is key.

### Framing Effect
How information is presented changes decisions. "90% survival rate" sounds different from "10% death rate."

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
We continue investing because of what we've already invested, not because of future value. Cut losses early.

### Status Quo Bias
We prefer things to stay the same. Change requires overcoming inertia — make change easy.

### Social Proof
We look to others for how to behave. If everyone is doing something, we assume it's correct. Break from the herd.

### Endowment Effect
We overvalue what we own simply because we own it. Be willing to let go.

# Influence — Robert Cialdini

## The 6 Principles of Persuasion

### 1. Reciprocity
Give before you ask. People feel obligated to return favors.

### 2. Commitment and Consistency
Small commitments lead to larger ones. Get a "yes" to a small request first.

### 3. Social Proof
Show that others are doing it. Testimonials, reviews, numbers.

### 4. Authority
People follow experts and credible figures. Build your authority through knowledge and presentation.

### 5. Liking
People say yes to people they like. Find common ground, be genuine, give compliments.

### 6. Scarcity
Things become more desirable when they're rare. Limited time, limited quantity, exclusive access.

# Emotional Intelligence — Daniel Goleman

## The 5 Components

### 1. Self-Awareness
Know your emotions as they happen. Recognize your strengths and weaknesses.

### 2. Self-Regulation
Control impulsive feelings and behaviors. Think before acting.

### 3. Motivation
Intrinsic drive to achieve for the sake of achievement. Not just money or status.

### 4. Empathy
Understand others' emotions. Put yourself in their shoes.

### 5. Social Skills
Manage relationships, build networks, find common ground.

# Applications for Business

## Negotiation
- Anchor high (or low) first
- Use silence as a weapon
- Frame offers in terms of their interests
- Build rapport before business
- Create urgency through scarcity

## Leadership
- Inspire through emotional connection
- Make people feel valued
- Lead by example
- Communicate the vision clearly
- Give feedback with empathy

## Sales
- Build trust through reciprocity
- Use social proof effectively
- Create urgency without being pushy
- Understand the buyer's psychology
- Tell stories, not statistics

## Marketing
- Tap into emotions, not logic
- Use scarcity and exclusivity
- Leverage social proof
- Frame benefits, not features
- Create compelling narratives
