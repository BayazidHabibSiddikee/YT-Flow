# Meditations — Marcus Aurelius (Key Passages)

## Book 1 — On Gratitude
When you arise in the morning, think of what a precious privilege it is to be alive — to breathe, to think, to enjoy, to love.

## Book 2 — On the Nature of Things
Begin each day by telling yourself: Today I shall be meeting with interference, ingratitude, insolence, disloyalty, ill-will, and selfishness — all of them due to the offenders' ignorance of what is good or evil.

## Book 3 — On Action
Never esteem anything as of advantage to you that will make you break your word or lose your self-respect.

## Book 4 — On the Inner Citadel
You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength.

## Book 5 — On Duty
At dawn, when you have trouble getting out of bed, present the thought: I am rising to do the work of a human being. Am I resentful that I was put here to do this?

## Book 6 — On Tranquility
The object of life is not to be on the side of the majority, but to escape finding oneself in the ranks of the insane.

## Book 7 — On Resilience
The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.

## Book 8 — On Perspective
How much more grievous are the consequences of anger than the causes of it.

## Book 9 — On Character
The best revenge is not to be like your enemy.

## Book 10 — On Self-Mastery
Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one.

## Book 11 — On Awareness
Very little is needed to make a happy life; it is all within yourself, in your way of thinking.

## Book 12 — On Mortality
Think of yourself as dead. You have lived your life. Now, take what's left and live it properly.

# Stoic Principles for Business

## The Dichotomy of Control
Some things are within our control, some are not. Focus only on what you can control: your thoughts, your actions, your responses.

## Amor Fati (Love of Fate)
Don't just accept what happens — love it. Use every obstacle as fuel. What stands in the way becomes the way.

## Memento Mori (Remember Death)
Remembering death clarifies priorities. Time is your most valuable resource — spend it wisely.

## The View From Above
When stressed, zoom out. See your problems from a cosmic perspective. Most of what worries you won't matter in a year.

## Premeditatio Malorum (Negative Visualization)
Imagine the worst-case scenario. When you've mentally prepared for disaster, you're free to act with courage.

## Virtue as the Only Good
The four cardinal virtues: Wisdom, Courage, Justice, Temperance. These are the only true goods. External goods (money, fame) are indifferent.

## Nature as Guide
Live according to nature — your rational nature. Think clearly, act justly, accept what comes.

# Business Applications of Stoicism

## Decision Making
- Control what you can, accept what you can't
- Make decisions based on virtue, not fear
- Think long-term, not short-term

## Leadership
- Lead by example, not by force
- Stay calm under pressure
- Put the team before your ego

## Resilience
- Obstacles become opportunities
- Failure is feedback, not defeat
- Stay disciplined when others panic

## Focus
- Eliminate distractions ruthlessly
- One task at a time, fully present
- Protect your attention like gold

## Negotiation
- Control your emotions
- Understand the other side's perspective
- Never react from anger or desperation
